Case study

Burst test support

Client
Iron ore slurry pipeline operator
Region
South America
Services
  • Pipeline integrity

Burst testing is an extremely valuable validation method for assessment methodologies. In this case, the pipeline operator has used burst tests to validate the API 579 calculated failure pressures.

Frontline Integrity have supported an iron ore slurry pipeline operator with the validation work, by performing calculations in line with API 579 Part 9 Level 2, as well as various sensitivities, to directly compare with the burst test results of various pipe spool sections.

01

Challenges faced by the customer

  • Burst testing of key pipe spools, with evidence of the primary integrity threat on the pipeline.
  • Results of the burst test provide key knowledge of the pipe materials and applicability of historically used assessment processes.
  • In depth knowledge of the assessment process required to provide adequate quantification of the safety margins which exist within the current assessment process.
  • An independent review was required to demonstrate that the results have been fully evaluated and the results can be integrated into the overall integrity management plans of the business.

The operator requested assistance from Frontline Integrity for support with the challenges above.

Background

The pipeline operator has been investigating the applicability of the API 579 Part 9 Level 2 methodology for assessing cracks identified in their pipeline during in-line inspections.

The validation works are an important aspect to demonstrate the models are appropriate to be used for future planning activities and provide confidence to the regulator that the work is representative of the pipeline and threat being analysed

02

The approach

Frontline Integrity received the burst test results in the form of written technical reports. These results included laboratory testing to identify existing anomalies within the pipe joints, which could then be directly compared to the failure analysis post-burst.

These anomalies which were present prior to burst testing were all assessed to API 579 Part 9 Level 2, using appropriate parameters for the pipe spools they are present within. These results provide a predicted failure pressure for each of the anomalies. The failure pressures could then be directly correlated to the actual failure pressure of the pipe spools within the burst test to quantify the levels of conservatism within the methodology.

The analysis showed a consistent level of conservatism within the assessment methodology, as shown in the unity plot below:

There was however one point which showed a closer unity between the predicted and actual failure of the pipe spools. This pipe joint was recommended for further investigation to confirm the exact cause of the lower-than-expected failure pressure.

The results showed a good correlation between the API 579 Part 9 Level 2 predicted failure pressure and actual burst pressure, with an appropriate level of conservatism. Further work on the pipe spool which failed with a lower-than-expected pressure was recommended.

Additionally, two of the pipe spools failed away from the reported anomalies. Using actual pipe properties (SMYS , UTS, etc), the failure pressure showed an almost direct correlation to that predicted by API 579 Part 9 Level 2.

03

Value added

Having a greater understanding of the assessments of anomalies in your pipeline is a massive result for integrity management plans. It allows for a more refined plan to be put into place, with focus on areas of uncertainty, to develop further knowledge. By performing, and subsequently validating the burst test, the pipeline operator has gained greater knowledge and confidence in the assessment results, which may result in less premature assessment failure and digs, saving time and money in the process.
